## Beyond the Hype Cycle

Every year brings a fresh list of "trends to watch." What's different about 2026 is how many of last year's experiments have become procurement requirements. Here are the shifts actually changing how businesses build and buy technology.

### 1. Edge AI

Running AI inference on-device or at the network edge — rather than round-tripping to the cloud — is now standard for latency-sensitive applications: point-of-sale systems, industrial sensors, and mobile apps that need to work offline. It also quietly solves a lot of data-privacy headaches, since raw data never has to leave the device.

### 2. Composable and Headless Architecture

Businesses are increasingly avoiding all-in-one platforms in favor of best-of-breed components — headless CMS, separate commerce engine, separate search — stitched together with APIs. It costs more to set up initially but avoids the lock-in and rigidity that monolithic platforms eventually impose.

### 3. Cybersecurity Becomes a Product Feature

Security is no longer a back-office concern bolted on before launch. In 2026, "how is data protected" is a top-three question in almost every enterprise sales cycle, and products are increasingly designed with security posture as a selling point, not an afterthought.

### 4. Low-Code/No-Code Matures

Low-code platforms have moved past simple internal tools into genuinely production-grade applications, particularly for workflow automation and internal dashboards. The best implementations combine low-code speed for the 80% of a product that's standard, with custom development for the 20% that's actually differentiated.

### 5. Sustainability as a Technical Requirement

Cloud cost optimization and carbon-aware infrastructure choices have converged — the same architectural decisions that reduce cloud spend (right-sizing, efficient caching, serverless where appropriate) also reduce energy use, and more RFPs are now asking for both numbers.
